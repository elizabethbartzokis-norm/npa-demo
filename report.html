Securities Act of 1933 — Registration Requirements
Federal — SEC
In Scope — Registration Required
ETF shares constitute securities and must be registered under the Securities Act of 1933. A registration statement on Form N-1A must be filed and declared effective before any public offering.
Analysis
The Securities Act of 1933 requires that any offer or sale of securities to the public be registered with the SEC unless an exemption applies. ETF shares are securities under Section 2(a)(1) of the Act. The MS Emerging Markets ESG ETF will offer shares to the public through authorized participants and secondary market trading, triggering full registration requirements. Form N-1A serves as the combined registration statement and prospectus for mutual funds and ETFs. The prospectus must include a complete description of the fund's investment objective, strategy, ESG methodology, risks, fees, and portfolio manager information. The SAI (Statement of Additional Information) must also be prepared. No exemption is available — this is a standard ETF registration requirement and is not a barrier to launch, but the registration process requires a defined drafting timeline and SEC review period of approximately 75 days.
Obligations
  • File Form N-1A registration statement with the SEC
  • Prepare and finalize prospectus including ESG methodology disclosure
  • Obtain SEC effectiveness declaration before any public offering
  • File annual updates and material amendments as required
Investment Company Act of 1940 — Fund Registration
Federal — SEC
In Scope — Fund Registration Required
The ETF must register as an open-end management investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940. Ongoing governance, reporting, and compliance obligations apply.
Analysis
The Investment Company Act of 1940 governs the organization and operation of investment companies, including ETFs structured as open-end funds. The MS Emerging Markets ESG ETF will pool investor capital and invest in a diversified portfolio of emerging market equity securities selected pursuant to an ESG methodology, meeting the definition of an investment company under Section 3(a)(1)(A). Registration as an open-end management investment company is required. The Act imposes ongoing obligations including board oversight requirements, custody requirements, affiliated transaction restrictions, and mandatory compliance program obligations under Rule 38a-1. Morgan Stanley's existing investment management infrastructure and compliance program substantially satisfy the framework requirements, reducing the incremental burden of this registration.

FINRA Rule 2111 — Suitability & Reg BI
Federal — FINRA / SEC
In Scope — Suitability Obligations Apply
Broker-dealer distribution of ETF shares triggers FINRA Rule 2111 suitability obligations and SEC Regulation Best Interest for retail investor recommendations.
Analysis
FINRA Rule 2111 requires that broker-dealers have a reasonable basis to believe a recommended security is suitable for a customer based on the customer's investment profile. SEC Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI), effective since June 2020, imposes a higher "best interest" standard for recommendations to retail customers, requiring broker-dealers to act in the retail customer's best interest at the time of a recommendation. The MS Emerging Markets ESG ETF will be distributed through Morgan Stanley's broker-dealer network, directly triggering both frameworks. The emerging markets mandate and ESG overlay introduce complexity relevant to suitability analysis — advisors must understand and accurately represent both the geographic risk profile (currency, political, liquidity risk) and the ESG methodology (index construction, exclusion criteria, potential tracking divergence from non-ESG benchmarks). Suitability profiles and training materials must be prepared before distribution commences.
Obligations
  • Prepare suitability profile and target investor documentation
  • Develop advisor training materials covering emerging markets risk and ESG methodology
  • Establish Form CRS disclosures for retail distribution channels
  • Document best interest analysis and conflict disclosure for Reg BI compliance
SEC Names Rule & ESG Disclosure Requirements
Federal — SEC
In Scope — Enhanced Disclosure Required
The ESG label in the fund name triggers the SEC's amended Names Rule, requiring 80% portfolio alignment with the ESG strategy and enhanced methodology disclosure in the prospectus.
Analysis
The SEC's amended Rule 35d-1 (Names Rule), effective September 2023 with a compliance date of December 2025, requires that any fund with a name suggesting a focus on a particular investment type — including ESG characteristics — invest at least 80% of its assets consistently with that focus. The "ESG" label in the fund name directly triggers this requirement. Additionally, the SEC has signaled through enforcement actions and staff guidance that ESG-labeled funds must provide clear, accurate, and non-misleading descriptions of their ESG methodology, data sources, and screening criteria. Greenwashing risk — the risk that ESG claims in marketing or fund documentation are not adequately supported by the actual investment process — creates both regulatory and reputational exposure. The fund's ESG methodology must be independently validated, clearly documented in the prospectus, and consistently applied. This is currently an open item and represents one of the highest-priority outstanding issues.
Obligations
  • Maintain 80% portfolio alignment with ESG investment criteria at all times
  • Document ESG methodology, data sources, and index construction rules in the prospectus
  • Complete independent validation of ESG scoring and exclusion methodology
  • Establish ongoing monitoring to ensure portfolio remains in compliance with ESG mandate
  • Implement annual review of ESG methodology against evolving SEC guidance

CFTC Commodity Pool Regulations
Federal — CFTC
Not Triggered
The ETF's investment strategy does not involve derivatives exposure above the de minimis threshold. CFTC commodity pool operator registration is not required.
Analysis
CFTC regulations require investment funds that trade commodity interests — including futures, swaps, and certain options — above a de minimis threshold to register as commodity pools, with the fund manager registering as a Commodity Pool Operator (CPO). The MS Emerging Markets ESG ETF is a long-only equity fund investing in emerging market equities selected pursuant to an ESG methodology. The fund's investment strategy does not contemplate the use of commodity derivatives, currency futures, or swaps above the CFTC's de minimis threshold (no more than 5% of fund assets in initial margin for commodity futures and options, or no more than 100% of liquidation value in commodity interests). Accordingly, CFTC commodity pool registration is not triggered. This determination should be revisited if the fund's investment strategy is modified to include derivatives exposure.
State Blue Sky Laws — Notice Filing Requirements
Multi-state
No Current Prohibition — Ongoing Monitoring Required
Registered ETF shares are generally covered securities exempt from state registration, but notice filings and fees are required in states where the ETF will be distributed.
Analysis
Under the National Securities Markets Improvement Act of 1996 (NSMIA), shares of registered investment companies are "covered securities" exempt from state securities registration requirements. However, states retain authority to require notice filings and fees as a condition of distribution within their jurisdiction. Most states require annual notice filings for ETFs distributed to their residents. The fund's distribution scope — initial targeted states for launch — must be confirmed and the corresponding state notice filings coordinated. Some states have enacted or are considering ESG-specific disclosure requirements for investment products marketed to retail investors; ongoing monitoring of state-level ESG regulation developments is required. No current state law prohibits the distribution of this ETF as currently scoped.
Obligations
  • Confirm initial target state distribution list and coordinate notice filings
  • Pay applicable state notice filing fees before distribution in each state
  • Monitor state-level ESG disclosure requirements as regulatory landscape evolves
  • Re-run regulatory applicability analysis if distribution scope expands

Explanation

Why ESG Disclosure Is the Highest-Priority Item: The SEC's amended Names Rule requires that 80% of the fund's assets be invested consistently with the ESG focus suggested by the fund name. Counterparty Risk — Critical Gap: Emerging markets custody involves sub-custodian relationships in jurisdictions where legal protections for fund assets may be weaker than in developed markets. Counterparty concentration limits for sub-custodian relationships are a fundamental risk control requirement. Without defined limits, the fund cannot evidence that its counterparty risk is adequately managed, which is a prerequisite for the NPA committee's risk acceptance.

Liquidity Risk — Rule 22e-4: SEC Rule 22e-4 requires registered funds to implement a liquidity risk management program that includes classification of portfolio assets into four liquidity buckets, designation of a minimum highly liquid investment percentage, and board oversight. Emerging markets equities present specific liquidity challenges — many constituents trade in markets with limited daily volume and extended settlement cycles. The liquidity risk management program must be tailored to the emerging markets mandate and cannot simply replicate the approach used for developed market funds.

Settlement Risk: Several target markets in the fund's index involve non-standard settlement procedures. Vietnam operates a T+2.5 settlement cycle with specific local broker requirements. Operations has identified these markets; validation of the settlement workflow must be completed and documented before the committee submission.
